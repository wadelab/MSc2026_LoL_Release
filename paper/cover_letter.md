Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit the enclosed manuscript, "Larks, Owls, and the Rift:
Circadian Rhythms in Global *League of Legends* Play," for consideration as a
Regular Article in EPJ Data Science.

The paper asks whether circadian rhythms — and the morning-lark/evening-owl
chronotype split familiar from chronobiology — are legible in large-scale
behavioural telemetry from online competitive gaming. Using approximately 92.7
million ranked *League of Legends* matches from about 8,000 high-ranked players
across eight regional servers, I construct per-player hourly time series and apply a
within-subject spectral pipeline: incoherently averaged Lomb–Scargle periodograms,
FDR-controlled phase estimation, and BIC-compared circular von Mises mixtures,
meta-analysed across servers. Behaviour proves strongly circadian (a best period of
23.96 h and 23.94 h for two behavioural principal components, with more than one in
five players individually significant), and the distribution of personal peak times
is bimodal — a data-driven echo of larks and owls. In contrast, competitive outcome
(the change in matchmaking rating) carries essentially no circadian signal, even for
the same players on the same games.

I believe the work is a good fit for EPJ Data Science. It is a computational
social science study built entirely on a large digital-trace dataset; it turns
naturalistic platform telemetry into a quantitative instrument for human
chronobiology; and the analysis pipeline is offered as a reusable template for
circadian questions in other large behavioural datasets. The de-identified derived
outputs and the analysis code are released openly to support reproducibility.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under
consideration elsewhere. It has a single author, who has approved the submission. I
declare no competing interests, and no specific funding supported the work. The raw
match records are proprietary to Riot Games and cannot be redistributed; the
manuscript includes a data-availability statement describing exactly what is shared
and the conditions governing the underlying data.

Thank you for considering this submission. I would be glad to provide any further
information.
